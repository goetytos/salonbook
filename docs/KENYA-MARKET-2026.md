# SalonBook Kenya market brief

Research date: 2026-08-12

## Market conclusion

Kenya is a credible mobile-booking market, but “online appointments” alone is
not a durable differentiator. Mobile money and WhatsApp/SMS are baseline local
expectations, while the first product advantage should be a fast branded
booking link, dependable availability, hands-on onboarding and reliable salon
operations.

The recommended first segment is an appointment-led, single-location Nairobi
salon, spa, nail studio, braider or premium barber with about 2–8 staff and an
existing Instagram or WhatsApp audience. Initially avoid medical aesthetics
(sensitive health data), large chains (multi-branch permissions) and
walk-in-heavy businesses (POS/offline/commission needs).

Recommended pitch:

> Give customers a branded booking link, keep one reliable live calendar, and
> reduce missed appointments without making customers install an app.

Do not claim SalonBook already supplies marketplace demand. Prove the merchant
workflow first, then decide whether consumer acquisition is worth funding.

## Evidence

- Kenya’s Communications Authority reported 83.5% smartphone penetration and
  97.3% 4G population coverage at June 2025. Feature phones remain relevant,
  supporting SMS as the broad fallback channel. [CA market update](https://www.ca.go.ke/mobile-internet-and-tech-services-surge-kenya-digital-shift-accelerates)
- CA reported 48.6 million mobile-money subscriptions at September 2025.
  [CA sector statistics](https://www.ca.go.ke/index.php/node/908)
- Safaricom reported 35.82 million monthly active M-Pesa customers and 675,860
  active Lipa na M-Pesa merchants for FY2025. [Safaricom FY2025 results](https://www.safaricom.co.ke/images/Downloads/FY25-Press-Release_May-9-2025.pdf)
- Safaricom describes Business Till as suitable for businesses including
  salons, and Daraja exposes sandbox and M-Pesa APIs. [Lipa na M-Pesa](https://www.safaricom.co.ke/lipa-na-mpesa/paying-to-businesses-and-online), [Daraja](https://developer.safaricom.co.ke/)
- CA’s audience study identified Facebook and WhatsApp among Kenya’s leading
  social platforms, making shareable links and mobile messaging important.
  [CA audience report](https://www.ca.go.ke/sites/default/files/2024-12/Audience%20Measurement%20and%20Industry%20Trends%20Report%20Q1%202024-2025.pdf)

## Competitive baseline

Public vendor claims were reviewed but not independently field-tested.

| Product | Public positioning | SalonBook implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Urembo](https://www.uremboapp.com/) | M-Pesa/cards, WhatsApp, inventory, commissions, accounting/eTIMS positioning; KES 2,999 per branch/month publicly shown | Closest Kenya-local feature/price benchmark |
| [PRIM](https://prim.co.ke/) | Appointments, walk-ins, POS, M-Pesa and SMS; tiers publicly shown around KES 1,500/4,500/9,500 | Local buyers already compare operational suites, not calendars alone |
| [Styled By Us](https://styledbyus.co.ke/) | WhatsApp self-booking, deposits and commission wallets | Messaging and deposits are an acquisition wedge |
| [Fresha](https://www.fresha.com/for-business/features) | Global marketplace, advanced scheduling, deposits, reminders, POS, inventory and loyalty | Long-term experience benchmark; SalonBook needs local focus, not feature imitation |

Pricing to test—not publish as settled pricing:

- Thirty-day guided pilot: free in exchange for weekly feedback.
- Booking Lite: KES 1,500–2,500 per branch/month.
- Growth with reminders/deposits: KES 2,999–4,500 plus message usage.
- Operations/POS/multi-branch: KES 8,000–10,000 later.

Validate willingness to pay with three pilots before fixing public prices.

## Kenya-specific product implications

1. **Messaging:** start with Africa’s Talking SMS for immediate confirmation,
   owner alert, cancellation and reminders. Add WhatsApp after opt-in, approved
   templates, delivery receipts and STOP handling are ready.
2. **Payments:** settle deposits directly to each salon’s own Till/Paybill via
   Daraja or a licensed PSP. Do not aggregate or redistribute customer funds
   without specialist advice. [National Payment System Act](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2011/39/eng%402025-11-04)
3. **Privacy:** SalonBook is likely a processor for salon customer records and a
   controller for platform accounts/security/marketing. Publish notices, sign a
   controller–processor agreement, decide ODPC registration, and document EU
   hosting safeguards. [Data Protection Act](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2019/24/eng%402022-12-31), [General Regulations](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2021/263), [ODPC FAQ](https://www.odpc.go.ke/faqs/)
4. **Tax:** salons remain responsible for customer tax invoices. SalonBook must
   issue compliant invoices for its own subscription fees. If SalonBook becomes
   the salon POS/invoicing system, establish the eTIMS integration boundary
   before calling it compliant. [KRA eTIMS](https://www.kra.go.ke/online-services/etims)
5. **Discovery:** capture an exact map location later; a no-key Google Maps
   directions URL already covers the pilot need. A Google Business Profile can
   link directly to appointment booking. [Google appointment links](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6218037)

## Order of execution

1. Controlled synthetic demo and structured salon interview.
2. One booking-only pilot with strong monitoring and human support.
3. Three pilots, four weeks each, then validate price and daily-workflow gaps.
4. M-Pesa deposits only after direct-settlement architecture and policies.
5. Walk-ins/POS/commissions/inventory only if pilot evidence makes them the
   highest-retention work.
